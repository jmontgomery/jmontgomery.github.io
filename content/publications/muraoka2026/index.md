summary: 'In linguistically diverse democracies, do political parties actually bother to speak minority languages — or do they just stick to the dominant tongue? This paper analyzes Facebook posts from more than 800 parties across 87 countries to build the first global map of multilingual party communication, and asks what drives some parties to reach across language lines while others don''t. The answer turns out to depend on both electoral rules and ideology — majoritarian systems and left-leaning parties both push harder toward multilingual appeals. Beyond party behavior, the study also shows that candidates nominated by multilingual parties are themselves more likely to campaign in minority languages — suggesting this is genuine representation, not just window dressing.'
