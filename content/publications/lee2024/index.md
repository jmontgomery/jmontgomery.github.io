abstract: ''
summary: 'Psychologists have long known that people tend to perceive minority and marginalized groups as more uniform and interchangeable than dominant groups — a phenomenon called homogeneity bias. This paper asks whether large language models like ChatGPT have inherited the same blind spot. The answer is yes: when prompted to generate text about racial and gender groups, ChatGPT consistently portrayed African, Asian, and Hispanic Americans as narrower in human experience than White Americans. The findings matter because AI systems that flatten the diversity within marginalized communities don''t just reflect existing stereotypes — they risk entrenching and amplifying them at scale.'
