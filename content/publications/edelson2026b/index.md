summary: 'Platforms and policymakers keep rewriting the rules for online political advertising, but what regulations do citizens actually want? Using a nationally representative NORC AmeriSpeak probability sample of U.S. adults, this paper measures public preferences across six policy domains that have driven real regulatory debates, from misinformation removal to microtargeting limits. Trust in platforms is low across the partisan spectrum, and bipartisan majorities support sponsor disclosure, limits on microtargeting, and holding news organizations to the same rules as everyone else. Partisan disagreement concentrates in a few domains, above all misinformation, where any platform choice, including inaction, will be contested.'
