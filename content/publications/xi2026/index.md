abstract: 'Abbreviated personality measures are practically important for personality research but sacrifice measurement precision, particularly at trait extremes, attenuating criterion validity in ways researchers often underappreciate. Computerized Adaptive Tests (CATs) address this by selecting items tailored to each respondent''s trait level to create more accurate measures, but uptake of these methods has been limited by a lack of thorough validation and accessible implementation tools. We introduce and validate the Big Five Adaptive Inventory (BF-AI), a freely available CAT built on the 100-item IPIP-NEO via the open-source catSurv R package. In a simulation study, the BF-AI outperforms fixed short forms (TIPI, BFI-2-XS, Mini-IPIP) in measurement precision across the full trait distribution, with just five adaptive items matching the accuracy of the full IPIP-50. In a criterion validity study (n = 3,029), the BF-AI predicted seven consequential life outcomes as well as or better than fixed short forms, frequently replicating full-scale effect sizes, demonstrating that adaptive assessment can substantially reduce the brevity–precision trade-off without increasing respondent burden.'
